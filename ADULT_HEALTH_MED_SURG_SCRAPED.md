## Sample Questions

### Question 1 (Diabetes + Steroids)
A client with type 2 diabetes mellitus managed on insulin is admitted for an acute Asthma exacerbation. The healthcare provider prescribes oral prednisone. What is the priority nursing intervention?

**Answer:** A) Monitor for signs of hypoglycemia
- Rationale: Prednisone (a corticosteroid) can cause hyperglycemia, but the priority is monitoring for hypoglycemia since the patient is already on insulin and steroids can affect glucose metabolism unpredictably.

### Question 5 (Numeric - Insulin Drip)
The nurse is administering an insulin drip for a patient in DKA who weighs 231 lbs. The order is to start the insulin drip at 0.1 units/kg/hr. Calculate the milliliters per hour the nurse will set the infusion pump.

**Answer:** 
- Convert weight: 231 lbs ÷ 2.2 = 105 kg
- Calculate dose: 105 kg × 0.1 units/kg/hr = 10.5 units/hr

### Question 21 (Insulin Mixing)
During a return demonstration, a nursing student prepares to mix NPH and Regular insulin. Which sequence of steps indicates correct technique?

**Answer:** B) Inject air into Regular, draw Regular, inject air into NPH, draw NPH
- Rationale: "Clear before cloudy" - Regular insulin (clear) is drawn before NPH (cloudy) to prevent contamination.

### Question 33 (DKA Priority)
A client arrives at the emergency department exhibiting polyuria, polydipsia, and polyphagia. Assessment findings include orthostatic hypotension, dry warm skin with decreased turgor, and acetone breath. What is the nurse's priority nursing diagnosis?

**Answer:** D) Fluid Volume Deficit
- Rationale: The classic DKA triad (polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia) plus signs of dehydration (orthostatic hypotension, dry skin, decreased turgor) indicate severe fluid volume deficit as the priority.

## Key Learning Points

### Diabetes Nursing Priorities:
1. **Hypoglycemia recognition and treatment** - 15g fast-acting carbs
2. **Insulin administration** - Rotate sites, proper storage, mixing technique
3. **DKA vs HHNS** - DKA: ketones present, HHNS: no ketones, higher glucose
4. **Sick-day management** - Continue insulin, monitor glucose frequently
5. **HbA1c** - Best indicator of long-term glucose control (3-month average)

### Parkinson's Disease Care:
1. **Bradykinesia** - Slow movements affecting ADLs
2. **Levodopa therapy** - Monitor for on-off syndrome, hallucinations
3. **Aspiration risk** - Use thick liquids, upright positioning
4. **Non-motor symptoms** - Depression, orthostatic hypotension, sleep issues
5. **Deep brain stimulation** - Reduces tremors and rigidity

### Multiple Sclerosis Management:
1. **Fatigue management** - Rest in cool environment, balance activity/rest
2. **Fall prevention** - Remove clutter, use adaptive devices
3. **Bladder control** - Timed voiding schedule
4. **Spasticity treatment** - Baclofen (don't stop abruptly)
5. **MS types** - Relapsing-remitting (most common), primary progressive (steady decline)
